# Viz.ai: Point Solution or Workflow Solution?

*Product strategy analysis*

## The Question
Viz.ai's primary entry point is AI-powered detection of time-sensitive conditions (stroke, pulmonary embolism, cardiac disease). But its broader product portfolio suggests it's solving a bigger problem: accelerating the entire care pathway from diagnosis to treatment.

## Job to Be Done
> When a patient presents with a time-sensitive condition, I want to quickly identify the condition, connect the right specialists, and coordinate next steps in care, so treatment can begin faster and outcomes improve.

Detecting a condition only creates value if it leads to timely clinical action — the job extends well beyond diagnosis.

## Point Solution vs. Workflow Solution
A **point solution** would mean AI-driven disease detection from medical imaging, full stop.

A **workflow solution** supports the full care journey:
`Disease Detection → Specialist Notification → Care Coordination → Treatment Decision → Patient Outcome`

Products like Viz One and Viz Assist show Viz.ai isn't limited to detection — it helps providers communicate findings, coordinate teams, and move patients toward treatment faster.

## Trade-offs

| | Point Solution | Workflow Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Sales & implementation | Easier | Harder |
| ROI | Clear, single use case | Distributed across stakeholders |
| Adoption | Faster | Slower |
| Competitive moat | Easy to replicate | Deeper integrations, stronger stickiness |

## Recommendation
**Sell as a point solution; build as a workflow solution.**

Hospitals adopt Viz.ai for a specific, measurable clinical use case — that's the wedge. But the platform's durable value, and its long-term competitive advantage, comes from care coordination across the full journey. AI detection is the entry point; care coordination is the moat.
