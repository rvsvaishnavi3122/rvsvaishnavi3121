# Swiggy Dineout — Offer Transparency

**"Users don't hate bad offers. They hate surprises."**

## The Problem
The issue on Swiggy Dineout isn't that discount information is hidden — it's that it arrives **too late**. Users see a headline discount ("Flat 30% off") on the restaurant page, commit through the booking flow, and only discover at the final step that just 10% applies to their selected slot.

### Current Experience
1. **Restaurant page** — user sees the highest available discount
2. **Booking flow** — selects date, guests, slot
3. **Reality check** — discovers only 10% is actually available for that slot
4. **Outcome** — expectation mismatch creates friction, disappointment, and higher drop-off

## The Fix
Set the right expectation **early**, not late.

### Better Experience
1. **Restaurant page** — shows a range ("Up to 30% off — offers vary by time slot")
2. **Booking flow** — user selects date/guests/slot seeing the *actual* discount per slot upfront
3. **Expected outcome** — no surprises, clear offer before commitment
4. **Outcome** — transparency builds trust and conversion

## PM Takeaway
Information isn't late because it's hidden — it's late because it's revealed *after* commitment. Teams often optimize for clicks into the funnel; users are optimizing for certainty. Every extra click spent discovering reality creates friction. Better products surface the right information at the right time, not just the most enticing headline.

**Open question:** optimize for CTR into the booking flow, or for booking completion rate? These two metrics can pull in opposite directions — a punchier top-line discount drives clicks but not conversions if it doesn't hold up at checkout.
